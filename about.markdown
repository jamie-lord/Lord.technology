I'm Jamie Lord, Solution Architect at CDS UK — the sole one, which means every significant technical decision crosses my desk and plenty of them end up in code review too.

Most of my time goes on Single Online Home, a national policing platform serving almost every UK police force. 160+ bespoke components, millions of daily interactions, sub-second response times, on a hybrid Azure / Cloudflare estate. That work sits at the level where architecture decisions matter to people who will never know my name, and I think that's exactly where it should.

A lot of my current attention goes on agentic engineering — how teams actually work with Claude Code as a first-class collaborator rather than a novelty. I authored CDS's practice around it in [our engineering handbook](https://handbook.cds.co.uk/): `CLAUDE.md` hygiene, TDD loops as agent guardrails, compound-engineering retrospectives, human review discipline under agent load. Alongside that I'm building MCP servers as the way enterprises productise their data assets for AI consumption, and I wrote the AI governance framework we use with public-sector clients (aligned to ISO 42001, most recently for the College of Policing).

Separately from the day job, I wrote [*Architecting on Cloudflare*](https://architectingoncloudflare.com) — a book about the Developer Platform (Workers, Durable Objects, D1, R2, Vectorize, Workers AI) for architects evaluating edge-native design against AWS, Azure, and GCP. It's in beta as of early 2026. The handbook is Azure-shaped; the book is where the edge-native half of my brain lives.

The arc here runs from mobile development through to enterprise architecture. I co-founded HeadClear in 2021 and spent two years as CTO building a privacy-preserving employee wellbeing platform from the ground up — ML models for anomaly detection, device-only vital-signs capture through facial blood-flow mapping, HIPAA-level data handling throughout. Before that I took Flexipay from a legacy desktop payroll product to a multi-tenant SaaS serving 3,500 UK organisations, including the whole HMRC RTI submission pipeline.

It's still pretty rare that I'm not toying with [a side-project or two]({{ '/projects/' | relative_url }}) ([see GitHub for more](https://github.com/jamie-lord)) — I particularly enjoy using Blazor to rapidly prototype, something I've been doing since 0.1.0 back in March 2018. When a piece of writing doesn't belong on a technical blog I put it at [nearlyright.com](https://nearlyright.com) instead. This site is, by choice, a Jekyll static site I shaped myself.
